## 2. The Problem: Field Operations Without Data

### How Field Operations Are Managed Today

On a typical construction site, event venue, or pilgrimage operation, hundreds or even thousands of workers are spread across large outdoor areas. Supervisors rely on paper attendance sheets, gate check-ins, phone calls, and WhatsApp groups to know who is on site, where teams are working, and what has been done.

This information is scattered, manual, and rarely stored in a structured way. At the end of the day, the organization is left with little reliable data about what actually happened on site: where each team spent its time, how long tasks took, how many hours were actually worked, and whether any worker was at risk.

### Why This Is a Problem

Without data, organizations cannot see or improve their own operations:

- **Decisions are based on estimates:** Managers decide on staffing, task distribution, and shift planning without knowing how resources were actually used.
- **Waste stays hidden:** Idle time, overstaffed or understaffed zones, and the gap between paid hours and hours actually worked go unnoticed, draining time, money, and effort.
- **Safety risks go unseen:** In extreme heat, fatigue and heat stress build up gradually. Without early signals, managers only find out when an incident has already happened.
- **Compliance is hard to prove:** Organizations are expected to follow regulations such as the midday outdoor work ban, but have no reliable records to demonstrate it to clients, auditors, or authorities.
- **Reporting consumes time:** Supervisors spend hours every day collecting, compiling, and sending manual reports instead of managing their teams.

### Why It Has Not Been Solved Yet

Tools for parts of this problem already exist, but most organizations still operate without complete field data because:

- **Solutions are fragmented:** Attendance systems, GPS trackers, and safety wearables each solve one piece, leaving organizations to connect the data themselves.
- **Some solutions are heavy to deploy:** Many require on-site infrastructure, which does not suit temporary sites, seasonal operations, or fast-moving projects.
- **Adoption barriers remain:** Cost, device durability, connectivity, and worker privacy concerns have slowed the adoption of field-data technology in the Saudi market.

The result is a large and growing field workforce managed with limited visibility, and a clear need for a single platform that records field operations and turns them into useful insight.

### 6.3 The Market Gap

The market today is split between three types of solutions:

1. **Comprehensive but heavy:** Construction workforce platforms offer strong features but often require on-site infrastructure and suit large, long-term projects.
2. **Specialized but narrow:** Heat-stress and safety wearables protect workers well but do not provide operational data for management decisions.
3. **Cheap but basic:** Generic trackers and attendance systems are affordable but offer little insight beyond location or entry records.

**The gap:** There is no affordable, easy-to-deploy platform that combines **location, health signals, tasks, and daily records in one place, and turns them into clear management insights**, while working on temporary and seasonal sites without on-site infrastructure.

### 6.4 Our Positioning

We position our platform as **the operational data layer for field workforces**: comprehensive enough to support management decisions, light enough to deploy in days, and designed around the Saudi context of heat, regulations, and seasonal operations.

Our competitive advantages:

- **Comprehensive data:** Location, vital signals, geofences, tasks, and records in a single platform.
- **Built for better decisions:** Analytics focused on cost, efficiency, and planning, not just safety alerts.
- **Fast, light deployment:** Off-the-shelf wearables with no on-site infrastructure required.
- **Local focus:** Designed for Saudi heat conditions, the midday work ban, and personal data protection requirements.
- **Accessible pricing:** A model suited to mid-size contractors and seasonal operations, not only giga-projects.

## 8. Business Model

### 8.1 Model Overview

Our business model is based on **Device-as-a-Service + Software-as-a-Service**. Instead of requiring clients to buy devices upfront, we offer a subscription that includes the wearable device, connectivity, and access to the platform. This lowers the initial cost barrier, which is one of the main obstacles to adopting field-data technology, and fits both year-round and seasonal operations.

### 8.2 Revenue Streams

| Revenue Stream | Description | Initial Pricing Assumption |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly Subscription** | Device, SIM connectivity, and platform access for year-round clients such as construction contractors | SAR 35–60 per worker per month |
| **Platform-Only Subscription** | Platform access for clients who purchase their own compatible devices | SAR 15–25 per worker per month |
| **Seasonal Rental** | Short-term packages for Hajj, Umrah, and events, including device delivery and collection | SAR 8–15 per worker per day |
| **Site Setup Fee** | One-time fee for site configuration, geofence setup, account creation, and training | Per site, based on size |
| **Analytics & Compliance Package** | Advanced reports, compliance records, and operational insights for management | Monthly add-on per company |

*All prices are initial assumptions to be tested with potential clients and adjusted after calculating device and connectivity costs.*

### 8.3 Illustrative Revenue Examples

- **Construction client:** 1,500 workers × SAR 40 per month = **SAR 60,000 monthly recurring revenue**, or **SAR 720,000 per year**, before add-ons.
- **Seasonal client:** 2,000 workers × 12 days × SAR 10 per day = **SAR 240,000 per season**.

*These are calculation examples only, not sales forecasts.*

### 8.4 Cost Structure

| Cost | Description |
|---|---|
| **Devices** | Purchasing wearables and replacing damaged or lost units |
| **Connectivity** | Monthly M2M SIM data plans from licensed local operators |
| **Cloud & Services** | Hosting inside Saudi Arabia, map APIs, and third-party analytics tools |
| **Operations & Support** | Device distribution, charging logistics, on-site setup, and customer support |
| **Compliance** | Legal consultation, data protection requirements, and device certification |
| **Team** | Development, sales, and operations staff |

### 8.5 Key Partners

- **Device suppliers:** Providers of certified GPS and health-sensor wearables
- **Telecom operators:** M2M SIM connectivity and IoT management portals
- **Cloud & map providers:** Hosting, mapping, and analytics services
- **Labor supply companies & contractors:** Channels to reach field workforces at scale

### 8.6 Why This Model Works

- **Low entry barrier:** Clients start without a large upfront investment in devices.
- **Recurring revenue:** Monthly subscriptions from construction clients provide stable income throughout the year.
- **Seasonal upside:** Hajj, Umrah, and events add high-volume revenue during peak periods.
- **Value-based pricing:** The cost per worker is small compared to the value of reduced idle time, fewer manual reports, and avoided incidents.

### 8.7 Future Revenue Opportunity

As the platform grows across clients and seasons, the aggregated data can support **anonymized industry benchmarks**, such as average idle time, heat-stress alert rates, or attendance patterns by sector. This would only be developed with clear client agreements and full compliance with the Personal Data Protection Law, and no individual worker data would ever be shared.

## 9. Go-to-Market Strategy

### 9.1 Approach

Our strategy is to **start small, prove value with real data, then scale**. Instead of targeting large contracts from day one, we begin with focused pilots, use their results as proof, and expand across segments following the natural calendar of the Saudi market.

### 9.2 Market Entry Phases

| Phase | Timeline | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0: MVP** | Oct – Nov 2026 | Build and test the MVP with real and simulated devices | A working, demo-ready platform |
| **Phase 1: Validation & First Pilot** | Dec 2026 – Mar 2027 | Interview potential clients and run a first pilot with a construction contractor or event operator | Prove the value of tracking, attendance, geofencing, tasks, and analytics with real data |
| **Phase 2: Hajj Showcase** | Around mid-May 2027 | Partner with a Hajj service provider for a high-visibility deployment | Demonstrate the platform at scale in extreme conditions |
| **Phase 3: Summer Construction Season** | Jun – Sep 2027 | Deploy with construction clients during peak heat and the midday work ban period | Prove the full value: heat-stress alerts, compliance records, and cost insights |
| **Phase 4: Expansion** | Late 2027 onward | Grow within existing clients and expand to facility management and municipal services | Recurring revenue and multi-site adoption |

### 9.3 Seasonal Calendar

| Period | Market Activity | What We Can Prove |
|---|---|---|
| **October – March** | Riyadh Season and winter events; mild weather | Tracking, attendance, geofencing, tasks, and operational analytics |
| **Around mid-May** | Hajj season | Large-scale temporary deployment in heat and crowded conditions |
| **15 June – 15 September** | Peak summer heat and the midday outdoor work ban | Heat-stress alerts, compliance records, and the full value of the platform |

*Note: Hajj moves about 11 days earlier each year, gradually shifting toward spring and winter months. Our Hajj value will therefore focus on safety, workforce management, and records rather than heat alone, while construction sites provide consistent summer heat every year.*

### 9.4 Pilot Offer

To lower the barrier for first clients, we offer a structured pilot:

- **Size:** 30–50 workers on a single site
- **Duration:** 2–4 weeks
- **Included:** Devices, connectivity, site setup, and dashboard access
- **Success metrics:** Data completeness, device battery life, worker acceptance, alert accuracy, time saved on reporting, and the gap between paid hours and hours actually worked
- **Outcome:** A results report the client can use to decide on a full rollout

### 9.5 Sales Channels

- **Direct B2B sales:** Reaching operations directors and HSE managers at construction contractors and giga-project subcontractors.
- **Labor supply companies:** Partnering with companies that supply workers to many sites, giving us access to large workforces through a single relationship.
- **Hajj & event partners:** Working through established Hajj service providers and event operators rather than approaching large authorities directly.
- **Telecom & technology partners:** Collaborating with telecom operators and device suppliers who already sell IoT solutions to enterprises.
- **Industry presence:** Participating in construction, safety, and technology exhibitions and events in the Kingdom.

### 9.6 Key Messages by Audience

| Audience | Our Message |
|---|---|
| **Operations Directors** | "See how your labor hours are actually spent, and make decisions based on data, not estimates." |
| **HSE Managers** | "Get early warnings before fatigue becomes an incident, with records that prove your site is safe and compliant." |
| **Site Supervisors** | "Stop chasing attendance sheets. See your whole team on one screen." |
| **Workers** | "This device is here to keep you safe, not to watch you." |

## 10. Regulatory Landscape

Operating a platform that collects workers' location and health data requires compliance with several Saudi regulations. We treat compliance as a core part of the product, not an obstacle, because the same regulations that govern us also create demand for reliable records and responsible data handling.

### 10.1 Key Regulations

| Regulation | Authority | What It Requires | Relevance to Our Platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal Data Protection Law — PDPL** | SDAIA | Lawful processing of personal data, explicit consent for sensitive data, data subject rights, breach notification, and restrictions on transferring data outside the Kingdom. Fully enforceable since 14 September 2024, with fines of up to SAR 5 million. | Worker location is personal data, and heart rate is health data, which is classified as sensitive. |
| **IoT Regulations** | CST | IoT devices must obtain a conformity certificate before being imported or used. SIM cards in IoT devices must be issued by CST-licensed operators, and foreign SIMs may only operate for up to 120 days. | Affects which devices we can use and how we manage connectivity. |
| **Cybersecurity Controls & IoT Guidelines** | NCA | Essential Cybersecurity Controls, Cloud Cybersecurity Controls, and recommended guidelines for secure IoT use. | Often required by government-related and giga-project clients. |
| **Midday Outdoor Work Ban** | MHRSD, with NCOSH | Prohibits working under direct sunlight from 12:00 to 15:00 between 15 June and 15 September. | Creates a need for clients to keep reliable compliance records. |
| **Medical Device Regulations** | SFDA | Products that claim to diagnose health conditions may be regulated as medical devices. | We present health readings as safety indicators only, not medical diagnoses. |

### 10.2 Our Data Protection Obligations

Because our platform involves systematic monitoring and sensitive health data, we expect the following PDPL requirements to apply:

- **Data Protection Officer:** Appointing a DPO, internally or through a third-party service.
- **Data Protection Impact Assessment:** Assessing risks before processing sensitive data or deploying new technology.
- **Registration:** Registering with SDAIA's national data governance platform.
- **Records of Processing:** Documenting what data we collect, why, how long we keep it, and who can access it.
- **Privacy Notice:** A clear privacy policy available in Arabic, and ideally in the languages of the workers.
- **Breach Notification:** Notifying SDAIA within 72 hours of discovering a data breach, and informing affected individuals without undue delay.
- **Data Processing Agreements:** Clear contracts with clients, where the client acts as the data controller and we act as the data processor.

### 10.3 How We Build Compliance Into the Product

| Principle | How We Apply It |
|---|---|
| **Data minimization** | Tracking only during working hours and within site boundaries |
| **Consent & choice** | Explicit consent for health data, with workers able to decline health monitoring while keeping other safety features |
| **Local hosting** | Hosting data inside Saudi Arabia to avoid cross-border transfer restrictions |
| **Access control** | Role-based access so each user only sees the data they need |
| **Audit & retention** | Logs of who accessed which data, and a clear retention and deletion policy |
| **Security by design** | Authentication, encrypted connections, and restricted database access from the first version |
| **Certified devices** | Using devices with CST conformity certificates and M2M SIMs registered under the company |
| **No medical claims** | Health readings are presented as safety indicators, not clinical measurements |
| **Transparency with workers** | Clear communication, in workers' languages, that the platform is for safety, not surveillance |

### 10.4 Compliance as a Competitive Advantage

Many organizations hesitate to adopt tracking technology because of privacy and regulatory concerns. By designing the platform around Saudi regulations from day one, we turn this barrier into a selling point: clients get a solution that helps them **protect their workers, prove their compliance, and handle data responsibly**.

## 11. Post-MVP Risks "Within 6 Months"

After the MVP, the platform moves from a development project to a real business operating in the market. The following table identifies the main business and technical risks expected within the first 6 months after the MVP, along with our mitigation strategies.

| Risk Category | Potential Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Market Competition** | **Established Competitors:** Existing workforce tracking and safety solutions already serve large projects, making it harder to win clients. | High | High | **Mitigation:** Focus on our differentiators: comprehensive operational data, analytics for management decisions, and fast deployment without on-site infrastructure. Target mid-size contractors and seasonal operations. |
| **Adoption** | **Worker Acceptance:** Workers may resist wearing devices due to privacy concerns or discomfort. | Medium | High | **Mitigation:** Communicate clearly that the platform is for safety, not surveillance. Track only during working hours and inside site boundaries. |
| **Legal & Compliance** | **Data Protection "PDPL":** Health data is classified as sensitive, which requires explicit consent and strict handling. | Medium | High | **Mitigation:** Seek legal consultation early, host data inside Saudi Arabia, and apply privacy by design through access control, audit logs, and a retention policy. |
| **Regulatory** | **Device & SIM Regulations:** IoT devices require CST conformity certificates, and SIM cards must come from licensed local operators. | Medium | Medium | **Mitigation:** Choose certified devices or work with suppliers who handle certification, and use M2M SIMs registered under the company. |
| **Sales** | **Long Sales Cycles:** Large contractors and government-related clients take months to approve new vendors. | High | Medium | **Mitigation:** Start with short pilot programs for a small number of workers, and target subcontractors and event operators with faster decision-making. |
| **Business Model** | **Seasonality & Cash Flow:** Demand from Hajj and events is seasonal, which creates uneven revenue. | High | Medium | **Mitigation:** Balance seasonal contracts with monthly subscriptions from construction clients. |
| **Market Shifts** | **Giga-Project Reprioritization:** Some giga-projects may be slowed, rescoped, or delayed, reducing expected demand. | Medium | Medium | **Mitigation:** Avoid dependence on a single project or client, and diversify across construction, events, and facility management. |
| **Seasonal Timing** | **Hajj Moving to Cooler Months:** Hajj shifts about 11 days earlier each year, reducing the role of heat in our Hajj value over time. | High | Low | **Mitigation:** Position our Hajj offering around workforce management, safety, and records, while relying on construction sites for consistent summer heat use cases. |
| **Hardware Operations** | **Device Cost, Loss & Damage:** Devices may be lost, damaged, or fail in extreme heat and dust. | High | Medium | **Mitigation:** Test devices in real summer conditions, and include loss and damage terms in client contracts. |
| **Data Quality** | **Inaccurate Readings:** Consumer-grade sensors may give unreliable heart rate readings, or lose signal in crowded or remote areas. | Medium | Medium | **Mitigation:** Present health readings as safety indicators, not medical values, and monitor data quality per device. |
| **Unit Economics** | **Thin Margins:** Device, connectivity, and support costs may be higher than expected, reducing profitability per worker. | Medium | High | **Mitigation:** Calculate real costs before finalizing pricing, negotiate volume deals with suppliers and telecom operators, and adjust pricing based on pilot results. |
| **Scalability** | **System Load:** Moving from a few test devices to hundreds or thousands of workers may overload the backend and database. | Medium | High | **Mitigation:** Plan a scalable architecture after the MVP using message queues, cloud hosting, and database optimization, and run load tests before large deployments. |

## 12. Validation Plan

Many parts of this study are based on research and assumptions. Before scaling, we need to validate them with real potential clients and real field data. The goal is to prove that the problem is real, that our platform solves it, and that clients are willing to pay for it.

### 12.1 Key Assumptions to Validate

| Assumption | What We Need to Learn | How We Will Validate It |
|---|---|---|
| **The problem is real** | Do organizations lack reliable field data, and does it affect their decisions and costs? | Client interviews |
| **Our value matters** | Which value matters most to clients: decisions, cost savings, safety, or compliance? | Client interviews and pilot feedback |
| **Willingness to pay** | Will clients pay for the platform, and which pricing model do they prefer? | Pricing discussions during interviews and after the pilot |
| **Worker acceptance** | Will workers wear the devices consistently during their shifts? | Pilot usage data and worker feedback |
| **Technical feasibility** | Do the devices work reliably in real site conditions: battery life, heat, and connectivity? | Field testing during the pilot |
| **Measurable impact** | Can we show time saved, hidden costs identified, and alerts that matter? | Pilot results report |

### 12.2 Client Interviews

- **Target:** 15–20 interviews
- **Who:** Operations directors, HSE managers, and site supervisors at construction contractors, Hajj and Umrah service providers, event operators, and labor supply companies
- **Approach:** Instead of asking "Do you want a tracking system?", we focus on how they manage their operations today and what data they are missing

**Interview Questions:**

1. How do you track attendance and working hours on your sites today?
2. How do you know where your teams are and what they are working on during the day?
3. How much time do your supervisors spend on daily reports?
4. How do you verify the hours billed by labor supply companies?
5. How do you manage worker safety during summer heat?
6. How do you prove compliance with the midday work ban to clients or auditors?
7. Have you used any tracking or safety technology before? Why did you continue or stop?
8. What would make workers accept or refuse wearing a device?
9. If you received one automatic report every week, what would save you the most time or money?
10. How would you prefer to pay for a solution like this: monthly, per season, or per project?

### 12.3 Field Pilot

Based on the pilot offer described in the Go-to-Market Strategy:

- **Size:** 30–50 workers on a single site
- **Duration:** 2–4 weeks
- **Measured during the pilot:**
  - Data completeness: percentage of expected readings received
  - Device battery life during real shifts
  - Worker acceptance: percentage of workers wearing devices throughout their shifts
  - Alert accuracy: useful alerts compared to false alerts
  - Time saved on attendance and reporting
  - The gap between paid hours and hours actually worked

### 12.4 Success Criteria

We will consider the validation successful if:

- The majority of interviewed clients confirm the lack of reliable field data as a real problem
- At least one pilot client completes the pilot and requests to continue or expand
- Workers wear the devices for most of their shifts during the pilot
- The pilot produces at least 3 concrete insights with clear value, such as time saved, hidden costs, or safety alerts that led to action
- At least one client agrees to a paid contract or a signed letter of intent

### 12.5 Validation Timeline

| Timeline | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Oct – Nov 2026** | Build the MVP and start early conversations with potential clients |
| **Dec 2026 – Jan 2027** | Conduct client interviews and refine the value proposition and pricing |
| **Feb – Mar 2027** | Run the first field pilot and publish a results report |
| **Apr – May 2027** | Use pilot results to secure a Hajj showcase partner and paying clients |
| **Jun – Sep 2027** | Validate heat-stress alerts and compliance records during the summer season |
